I · Public & Spatial

Playful Knowings · Placemaking and play in a Northern Cree community

âniskômohcikewin

Weaving contemporary theories on play and placemaking with Cree practices of gathering to re-envision cultural and community revitalization on reserve. A gathering place for all ages and shaped with the community it is designed for.

Location
A Northern Cree community, Treaty 5
Context
Master of Interdisciplinary Design thesis, Emily Carr University of Art + Design
Status
Completed December 2024
Project type
Design research thesis, conceptual gathering place
Role
Designer and researcher
Methods
Two-eyed seeing, land-based observation, interviews, Creative Reflections workshops

The project

Playful Knowings is a Master of Design thesis and case study in my member First Nation community. It proposes âniskômohcikewin, a conceptual intergenerational gathering place on the Treaty Grounds, shaped by land-based observation, interviews with Elders and community members, and workshops in which participants redesigned everyday objects around their own sense of joy.

Significance

Using Elder Albert Marshall’s “two-eyed seeing,” Indigenous methodologies of relationality, situating and reciprocity and culturally appropriate participatory design, the thesis argues that a playful, Indigenous-led practice of designing outdoor gathering places on reserve can strengthen a community’s sense of identity and well-being. It favours a community-led practice that recognizes the strengths, assets and cultural abundance of First Nations.

How it was made

  1. Land-based observationIn-person time in the community, including major cultural events across the summer and winter of 2023–2024.
  2. InterviewsConversations with community members and Elders who engage in play, healing and placemaking practices.
  3. Creative Reflections workshopsThree workshops in which participants playfully redesigned everyday objects to reflect their own sense of joy, as a way to re-envision an outdoor gathering place.

Interview reel: community members on play, gathering and healing.

Findings

Six characteristics of a playful gathering place on reserve

In no particular order. Together they describe an interpretive space that doesn’t need complex architecture, a formal program or even four walls and a roof.

Agency

Self-led exploration and expression. The place affords high-energy activity like conversation, play and teachings, or low-energy activity like resting near others.

Spiritual & cultural practice

A meeting place for relatives, children, Elders, knowledge keepers and land-based practitioners. Social buffers like a community fire, coffee or smudging help people get acquainted.

Interconnection

Intergenerational: connecting children, youth, adults, Elders, spiritual ancestors and the natural environment, while reflecting on future generations.

Familiarity & proximity

Close to existing landmarks and places of gathering, carrying the cultural character of the community and land so it feels familiar and safe.

Accessible

Few physical barriers such as fences, gates or hours. Built for the climate and for community-level maintenance, so habits can form.

Enjoyable

Everyday joy: contentment, laughter, teasing, reconnecting. Part of shedding internalized colonial oppression as self-healing.

Conceptual drawingsA place named for connection

The conceptual gathering place is named âniskômohcikewin, the Cree word meaning connections. It is an informed, creative embodiment of the six characteristics.

It sits on the Treaty Grounds, slightly east of the land-based cabin, in three parts. At the top, curved benches of natural lumber make three semi-circles around a central firepit, descending into slat walkways that mark the points of entry. The centre structure has ten panels holding materials that respond differently to the year-round environment, with benches along its sides. In the last section, repurposed river rocks offer places to sit apart or together. It is roughly 30 metres long, 5 wide and 6 high.

It is also a joyful picture of the community’s fishing history: people and animals move into the narrow mouth of the central structure like fish, inspired by fishing boats and traps, then exit around the rapid rocks into the metaphorical river. The wide mouth faces the real river, about 150 metres away.

The exhibition

Playful Knowings was exhibited at the Libby Leshgold Gallery in Vancouver in May 2025, with folding photo cards, handmade books, interview videos, the community map and the conceptual drawings.

Outcome

The research found an active interest among community members in supporting a place for healing in a growing yet fragmented community, and weighed the strengths and limits of applying Western and Indigenous worldviews to designing outdoor places for gathering. The six characteristics and the conceptual drawings offer a starting point for that place: one that first fosters a sense of safety and well-being, then leads to a sense of play and enjoyment.
